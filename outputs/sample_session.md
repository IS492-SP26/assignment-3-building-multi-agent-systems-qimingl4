# Sample Session — Accessible UI Design

**Query**: What are the key principles of accessible user interface design?

## Final synthesised answer

Accessible user interface design rests on four widely cited principles, often called **POUR**, codified in the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG): perceivability, operability, understandability, and robustness [Source: W3C WCAG 2.2 Overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/).

1. **Perceivable** — information and UI components must be presentable in ways users can perceive, including text alternatives for non-text content, captions for media, and sufficient colour contrast (Henry, 2024).
2. **Operable** — components and navigation must be operable via keyboard, with generous time limits and no flashing content that can trigger seizures [Source: WebAIM accessibility primer](https://webaim.org/intro/).
3. **Understandable** — text must be readable, page behaviour predictable, and forms must offer clear error identification and recovery (Petrie & Bevan, 2009).
4. **Robust** — content must be robust enough to be interpreted by current and future user agents, including assistive technology — a key driver of ARIA semantics (Lazar et al., 2017).

In HCI research, perceivability and operability are most studied; understandability remains under-explored, especially for novice or cognitively disabled users.
